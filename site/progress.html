Status as of 3 October 2026 · one laptop (RTX 3060, 6 GB)

Where we are

The goal: reasoning that trains on a laptop and runs on a phone, without huge pretraining. The approach: small models that hold a problem in a hidden (latent) state and refine it in place, check their own answers, and spend extra effort only where they are unsure. Everything below was measured on problems the models never trained on.

Same 233k parameters. Thinking alone 7.5% → 48%; with search 38% → 72%. It now solves puzzles needing 50+ backtracks without search.

Energy-based reasoner

Solve by descending a learned energy. Solved 8% of small mazes and none larger, and its learned checker lost reliability on bigger mazes.

Thinking longer when unsure: +8 points on large mazes at no cost on easy ones. Searching when unsure doubled 41×41 but broke correct answers, because the model trusted itself on mazes it had never seen.

Learning new rules from a few examples (ARC)

A small settling network trained on each task's 2–5 examples solved 1 of 114 unseen tasks; every simple baseline solved none. It usually memorised the examples instead of learning the rule.

◐ Weak

Pocket runtime: the model at 4 bits, on a fast engine

Same 1.5B model, 1.1 GB instead of 3.1 GB: 10.7× faster and 5× less energy per correct answer (83 J), with no accuracy lost. Most of the old slowness was software overhead.

A fixed random code plus one closed-form solve. It never forgets earlier tasks, and on ARC it solved 3 of 114 unseen tasks, our best, in 14 seconds instead of 34 minutes.

✓ Worked

What we've learned

Training time is the cheapest lever

The same small model went from 7.5% to 48% just by training for 3 hours instead of 10 minutes.

A checker turns guesses into answers

With an exact check, search added 24 points on Sudoku and never returned a wrong answer.

Knowing when you've failed is trainable

"No path" took 13 minutes to learn, and it is what made the model's own search trustworthy.

A search needs a judge it can trust

Self-confidence failed on problems the search itself created. On code, the visible test is right 90% of the time.

Try several beats fix one

For small models, sampling and checking (+20.6 points) far outperformed repairing from error messages (+2.7).

What you learn from matters

Self-training on problems it already solved taught nothing. The frontier is where learning happens.

Fast learning doesn't need backprop

One closed-form solve beat gradient training at learning ARC rules, about 150× faster, and it never forgets.

Roadmap

1. Verify and retry on real code

✓ Done

Repair gave +2.7 points; best of 8 with the visible test gave +20.6. The visible test is a useful but imperfect judge (90%).

2. Learn from its own verified work

⏸ Paused

Round 1 showed no gain. Round 2 targets the frontier and is 9 of 139 problems into its first step, ready to resume when the laptop can stay plugged in with the lid open for about 4 hours.

3. Think in latent space, not tokens

◐ Started

Proven on mazes and Sudoku (the Arena scorecard). On ARC, backprop-free fast learning is ahead but still at 2.6% of unseen tasks; richer, object-level codes are next.

4. Run it small, everywhere

● In progress

Week 1 done: the code model runs locally at 4 bits, 0.5 s and 83 J per correct answer. Next: a router that spends more effort only when a cheap answer fails its check, then the phone.
